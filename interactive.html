01 — Premise & Structural Irony

A giant chasing the sun, a hut snagged on his chain

A prideful War God descends to reclaim humanity’s primordial fire. Blinded by hubris, he assumes something so precious must be colossal—so he races eastward staring only at the horizon to seize the rising sun, crushing valley hearths into ash beneath his stride. High on a barren ridge sits a lowly thief’s wooden shack keeping the last undying ember; because the hilltop stands level with the giant’s rusted armor chains, a swinging link accidentally hooks the roof and tears the cottage loose like a dangling lantern.

Gameplay unfolds as a symmetrical two-phase run: an upward Ascent through branching ruins (balancing resource gathering, tool durability, and survivor rescues) pivoting at the hanging cottage into a high-velocity downward Retribution, where earlier choices between solidarity and greed directly reshape which escape paths stand open.

02 — Coupled Systems & Production Governance

Designing mechanics that constrain scope while deepening tension

Small-team interactive media fails when ambitious lore outgrows production capacity. Instead of multiplying disconnected features, the pre-production package ties a compact set of 2D systems together through explicit rules and cost boundaries:

1. Spatial Inventory & Active Burden Rule

An expandable 4×4 rotatable grid coupled directly to movement physics, chain-climbing thresholds, and emergency mouse-drag discard (Panic Jettison). Relics grant hazard resistance only while carried as live backpack weight—never from safe camp storage.

2. Three-Second Deduction & Camp Risk

Shattered prisons mix innocent villagers with disguised pickpockets and saboteurs. Players read subtle visual cues before spending crowbar durability to rescue; misjudgments carry lasting camp consequences, such as losing gear-repair support or stored gold.

3. Director’s Shot Cards & Diegetic Audio

Standardized scene cards specify 45° high-angle camera framing, three-plane silhouette staging, frame-by-frame beat timing, a high-contrast 16-bit noir palette (charcoal, bone, crimson, and a single warm lantern glow), and diegetic sound shifts—giving writer and pixel artist a frictionless handoff.

4. Scope Management, Coupling & Feasibility

Companion documentation models quantitative systemic coupling across weight, stamina, tool wear, and camp economy, alongside engineering justification and scope cut-lines calibrated to a two-person Godot 4 production entering greybox prototyping.

03 — Visual Direction Artifact: Scene 01 (“The Snag”)

Translating a written Director’s Shot Card into visual staging

To test cross-discipline communication before asset production, Scene 01 was written as a structured shot card establishing a two-tier 45° high-angle composition: darkened valley dwellings below, jagged silhouette rock in the foreground, the thief’s isolated ridge hut holding the frame’s sole warm golden window on the right third, and a massive rusted armor chain cutting in from the upper left as ambient night sounds drop to sudden silence.
