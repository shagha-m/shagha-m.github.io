Open by design: unlike the closed-source Homat DRM products, HoSub is a general production tool with no protected content inside it — so it is published in full, and reviewers can run it themselves. Zero installation dependencies, zero API keys, fully offline.

01 — Production Problem

Generic subtitle tools fail code-switching teachers

Language-lesson video is not ordinary speech: a teacher explains in one language, performs a phrase in the target language, reads it aloud, and switches back — sometimes three times in one sentence. Generic subtitling and transcription tools flatten all of that into a single-language transcript, losing exactly the parts a learner needs to study: the target phrases, their readings, and their audio.

I specified HoSub around that editorial reality: detect and style the target-language segments, keep readings (Pinyin / Romaji / romanization) attached to them, and turn every taught phrase into reusable study material instead of disposable captions.

The immediate trigger was the failure of the standard tool for this job: in general-purpose subtitle editors, a single sentence mixing words from several languages came back garbled and unusable, there was no way to group subtitles on the timeline and shift them together, and nothing connected the finished subtitles to the flashcards and printed handouts our courses ship with. HoSub closes exactly those gaps: code-switched lines stay intelligible and styled per language, cue groups move as one on the waveform timeline, vocabulary exports to a structured Excel workbook that feeds our flashcard products, and subtitle text exports into the Persian handouts and workbooks that accompany every video course.

It also had to work with no internet at all: course preparation runs on a fully local Whisper large model, because production could not depend on connectivity that might not be there tomorrow.

02 — What the Studio Does

From raw lesson video to subtitles, flashcards and audio in one pass

Premiere-Style Subtitle Timeline

Waveform timeline editing with live styled overlay — target-script colour, inline or stacked readings, and RTL BiDi punctuation locking for Persian-facing materials — plus cue grouping, locking, snap-to-voice and word-timestamp-aware trimming.

Offline Transcription

Local Whisper.cpp transcription with word-level timestamps, live progress and engine logs. No uploads, no API keys: unreleased lesson media never leaves the producer's machine.

Vocabulary & Audio Exports

Automatic deduplicated flashcards (word, reading, meaning, level, frequency, context, timestamp) exported to Excel / CSV / JSON, and a code-switching audio cutter that slices each taught phrase into a named clip library delivered as one ZIP.

Editorial Lexicons

Bundled offline dictionaries (HSK 1–9, Unihan, TOPIK, JLPT) plus a custom dictionary manager, so level labelling follows recognised curricula rather than guesswork.

03 — Why It Sits in This Portfolio

Localization is a media-management decision

HoSub is the production-side counterpart to the rest of this portfolio: literary translation deals with one text at a time, the Homat app distributes finished media, and HoSub turns that middle stage into a systematic, repeatable process — turning recorded teaching into subtitled, searchable, reusable multilingual assets without per-seat SaaS costs or cloud exposure. For independent language-media producers, that cost and privacy profile is the difference between localizing and not localizing at all.

04 — My Role & Method

Product leadership, honestly framed

As with the Homat products, my contribution was the product work: defining the code-switching editorial model, the subtitle styling and reading rules, acceptance criteria and the release itself — directing implementation, testing against real multilingual lesson recordings, and publishing the result openly so the workflow claims in this dossier can be checked by running the tool.
