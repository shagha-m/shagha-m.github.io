01 — Media Economics Problem

Why off-the-shelf LMS delivery falls short for independent publishers

In markets where unauthorized file sharing of video lessons, PDFs, and study decks is widespread, small academies and independent instructors face an acute unit-economics problem: months of editorial and instructional production can be redistributed hours after launch, while enterprise DRM suites remain prohibitively expensive or poorly adapted to local learners.

On Homat Academy, standard WordPress LMS plugins worked well for public course pages and structured syllabus display, but lacked controlled content protection tailored to our courses and study materials.

02 — Three-Component Product Ecosystem

Separating public catalog discovery from rights-managed delivery

Working alongside my brother and co-founder—and iterating through over a dozen production releases driven by live student feedback and structured acceptance testing—we organized the video-course DRM into three coordinated components while keeping WordPress/Tutor LMS strictly as the public syllabus layer:

1. Desktop Content Packager (“Locker”)

Instructor-side Windows application that inspects lesson media, packages encrypted course containers with multilingual filename support (including Persian and East Asian scripts), verifies output integrity on disk, and provides plain-language operator diagnostics.

2. WooCommerce License Server & Learner CRM

WordPress/WooCommerce management plugin automating entitlement issuance at checkout, enforcing per-course device-binding policies across desktop and mobile slots, and giving administrators an audit log and learner support CRM.

3. Protected Windows & Android Players

Native desktop (C++/Qt) and Android playback clients enforcing real-time online access verification, OS-level screen-capture blocking, learner watermarking, runtime tamper checks, and security-event reporting.

4. Companion E-Book & Flashcard DRM (“Homat Book”)

Applies the same rights-management philosophy to written educational publishing—protecting digital coursebooks and structured language flashcard decks across desktop and mobile devices.

03 — Live Governance, Graduated Enforcement & B2B Roadmap

Policy in practice across 36 issued licenses

As captured on 19 November 2026, the live Homat DRM & CRM console governs 36 issued course licenses and 18 active hardware-bound devices (HWID) across 5 protected courses (supporting an academy catalog of 197 lessons, 47 registered students, and 64 course enrollments).

Effective rights management is as much an editorial and policy discipline as a technical one. Rather than relying on hair-trigger automated bans that punish accidental user behavior, we operate a graduated enforcement policy backed by anti-capture audit logs: for instance, when telemetry recorded a video screen-capture attempt on an active account, we issued a formal first-offense warning explaining platform terms; when the audit log later captured two subsequent attempts to record course audio on the same account, the associated course licenses were formally revoked in accordance with Homat Academy’s published policy.

Running this full governance cycle inside Homat Academy serves as a real-world B2C proving ground across Windows and Android. The next phase evolves both the video-course DRM suite and our companion e-book and flashcard DRM into a commercial B2B offering for language institutes and independent instructors.
